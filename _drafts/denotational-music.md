Music and programming have a deep correlation.
Many programmers play musical instruments, or fancy writing music generation systems.
The same can be said about mathematicians (at least in my experience).
What is it about the music craft that appeals to this kind of minds?
In comparison with other arts and crafts, western music practice (and surely in most music traditions) is embelished with formal structures
that are used for the writing, communication, discussion and discovery of sound patterns that are salient to perception. 
Music is not just sound, and cannot be reduced to some structural representation.
For example, both silence and noise can be made into music by act of perception.
Nonetheless, I marvel at the kind or structure and *forms* that we can find in what we commonly call music.
This has been leveraged implicitly by LLMs to generate music compositions that sound plausible, or even enjoyable.
It makes sense that patterns emerge from the floods of data because patterns are used for their creation in turn. 
It is a matter of taste: some people cannot stand the beat of a drum machine, and much prefer a funky drummer.

What is music? Is a questionable question. It is something that can easily fallback to *for who?*.
Funny enough, music is said to be a universal language.

Programming itself depends on programming languages, and programming patterns.
Such pattern emerge, and as our understanding refines, they end up in abstractions that seem to solve different kinds of problems.
Music is not problem, but it turns out that, when subjected to problem-solving tools, interesting things emerge.

After experiencing Tidal Cycles live coding, I wondered about how such a compositional paradigm could come into being.
