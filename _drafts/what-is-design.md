The last couple of weeks have been characterized by a lot of though about the concept and practice of "design" in general,
and what does it mean in the context of software engineering.
Also, after some time of research in the space of split ergonomic programmable keyboards. new ZSA Voyager keyboard



The concept of software design has felt somewhat difficult to grasp for me since I first learned about it.
My first question on it was, how is it different from software architecture?
Might be wrong about this, but it seems to me that a first distinguishing aspect is a matter of scale.
If "architecture" focuses on what components integrate a system, and why are they necessary.together in a system

A rather mundane concern, I was choosing between to competing product for a keyboard.
Both resemble each other a lot, and in paper one of them exceed the other.
But on a closer look, I realized that their design addressed differing concerns.

I'll spare you the details on my deep dive on the feature of both devices (there are plenty of reviews online you could look at).
Bottom line: If you tend to work at different locations you can't go wrong with the Go60.
Given its price tag and list of features, it is probably the best the market has to offer in regard to a portable split keyboard.
Nevertheless I went with the Voyager, mainly because:

- ZSA company values, of which they are quite outspoken, resonate with me.
  Their take on [sustainability](https://www.zsa.io/sustainability) of their products shines.
- I like the minimal approach to the product. No bluetooth and battery and a plus for me.
- Their software support seems more advance.
